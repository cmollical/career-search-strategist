# Methodology

## Purpose

This reference explains the operating philosophy behind the Career Search Strategist.

The system is intended for deliberate career moves, especially searches where the candidate is seeking a meaningful change in level, scope, compensation, or trajectory.

## The search sequence

### 1. Establish the evidence

Before deciding what the candidate should pursue, understand what they have actually done.

Extract:

- career chronology
- formal titles
- actual operating scope
- leadership scope
- measurable business outcomes
- customer outcomes
- transformation work
- technical and analytical fluency
- cross-functional influence
- executive exposure
- recognition
- recurring strengths
- recurring development themes

The purpose is not to create flattering language. It is to establish the strongest defensible version of the candidate's record.

### 2. Define the move

A job search becomes much more efficient when the candidate can describe what would make a move worthwhile.

Define:

- target level
- desired scope
- compensation objective
- functional lanes
- industry preferences
- company-stage preferences
- remote/location constraints
- travel tolerance
- people-leadership preference
- risk tolerance
- non-negotiables

### 3. Build the market narrative

Translate a career history into a repeatable problem-solving identity.

The narrative should answer:

- What kind of problems does this person solve?
- At what scale?
- How do they solve them?
- What outcomes have they created?
- What is the logical next level?

### 4. Build target lanes

Use primary, stretch, adjacent, and avoid lanes.

This prevents title-driven searching and helps the candidate understand which opportunities deserve attention.

### 5. Establish economic guardrails

A move should be evaluated against the candidate's financial objective before emotional investment becomes high.

Separate dependable cash from speculative compensation.

### 6. Create access

At senior levels, access often determines which opportunities become real.

Use:

- existing relationships
- former colleagues and managers
- referrals
- peers in target roles
- adjacent leaders
- hiring managers
- selective recruiter relationships
- targeted applications

### 7. Package the proof

The candidate should have a small set of strong cases that prove the narrative.

A strong case explains the business problem, mandate, complexity, operating model, decisions, influence, measurable result, and learning.

### 8. Evaluate opportunities deliberately

A role can be attainable and still be a bad move.

Evaluate scope, compensation, level integrity, leadership, company health, work model, trajectory, and actual interest.

### 9. Run a pipeline

Every live opportunity should have:

- stage
- score
- contact
- next action
- next-action date
- unresolved questions
- risks

### 10. Compound learning

The search should get better over time.

After interviews and networking conversations, update:

- positioning
- story selection
- compensation assumptions
- target companies
- objections
- market language
- role-level understanding

Do not repeatedly make the same pitch if the market is giving useful contrary evidence.

## Seniority translation

Formal title is evidence, but it is not the only evidence.

Assess seniority using:

- decision authority
- organizational scale
- strategic ownership
- ambiguity
- people leadership
- executive interaction
- financial impact
- customer impact
- cross-functional breadth
- technical complexity
- program scale

The purpose is to identify the level the candidate can credibly compete at, not manufacture a promotion through wording.

## Search portfolio

Do not make the search dependent on a single title.

A healthy portfolio can contain:

- high-probability roles at the desired level
- credible stretch roles
- adjacent roles with unusually strong scope
- a small number of high-upside opportunities

Avoid filling the pipeline with roles the candidate would not actually accept.

## Decision principle

The correct question is rarely:

> Can I get this job?

The more useful question is:

> If I get this job, does it accomplish the move I set out to make?
