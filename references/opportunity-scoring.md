# Opportunity Scoring

## Purpose

The scorecard prevents excitement, brand prestige, or title from silently replacing the candidate's actual search criteria.

## Default weights

| Dimension | Weight |
|---|---:|
| Role and scope fit | 20% |
| Compensation | 20% |
| Level integrity | 15% |
| Leadership quality | 15% |
| Remote/location fit | 10% |
| Company health | 10% |
| Growth opportunity | 5% |
| Mission/product interest | 3% |
| Network access | 2% |

Total: 100%.

Adjust weights when the user has different priorities.

## Rating scale

### 5 - Excellent
Strong evidence this dimension exceeds the user's objective.

### 4 - Strong
Meets the objective with limited concerns.

### 3 - Acceptable/unclear
Could work, but material questions remain.

### 2 - Weak
Meaningful mismatch or risk.

### 1 - Poor
Direct conflict with the user's objective.

## Weighted score

For each dimension:

`rating / 5 × weight`

Sum all weighted values to produce a score out of 100.

## Decision bands

These are defaults, not rigid rules.

- **85–100:** high-priority pursuit
- **75–84:** strong opportunity, resolve key questions
- **65–74:** selective pursuit only if upside is compelling
- **Below 65:** likely pass unless important information is missing

## Confidence

Also rate confidence in the score.

A 90/100 opportunity based on guesses is not truly a 90.

Use:

- High confidence
- Medium confidence
- Low confidence

List the unknowns most likely to change the score.

## Hard constraints

A weighted score should not override a true non-negotiable.

Examples:

- required relocation when the candidate will not relocate
- compensation below an explicit minimum
- unsustainable travel
- role cannot legally employ the candidate in their location
- function fundamentally differs from the work the candidate wants

Flag hard-constraint failures separately.
