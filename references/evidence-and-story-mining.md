# Evidence and Story Mining

## Evidence hierarchy

Prefer concrete evidence over adjectives.

Strong evidence includes:

1. measured business outcomes
2. measured customer outcomes
3. scale metrics
4. organizational scope
5. documented responsibilities
6. manager or stakeholder corroboration
7. recognition or external credibility
8. technical artifacts or systems built
9. qualitative outcomes with clear context

"We saved $20M" is stronger than "results-oriented."

"Led an organization of 80" is stronger than "strong people leader."

## Evidence extraction

For each source document, look for:

- numbers
- before/after states
- ownership language
- scale
- decision rights
- cross-functional partners
- systems created
- recurring programs
- customer impact
- financial impact
- executive visibility
- awards and recognition
- manager descriptions of scope
- areas of improvement

Do not treat all statements as equally reliable. A metric repeated across multiple sources has stronger corroboration than a vague self-description.

## Evidence ledger rules

Every major resume or interview claim should be traceable when practical.

Use confidence:

- **High:** directly stated in a reliable source or corroborated across sources
- **Medium:** strongly implied but not fully documented
- **Low:** plausible but requires confirmation

Use sharing status:

- **Resume-safe:** suitable for public or semi-public career materials
- **Interview-safe:** suitable for private recruiting conversations
- **Private:** useful for strategy but should not be shared externally

## Story selection

Select stories that collectively demonstrate range.

A useful story bank may cover:

- transformation
- strategic leadership
- people leadership
- influence without authority
- ambiguity
- technical/analytical depth
- customer impact
- financial impact
- failure or course correction
- prioritization
- executive communication

Do not create ten stories that all prove the same thing.

## Story structure

Use:

**Situation → Mandate → Complexity → Actions → Operating model → Result → Learning**

The "Operating model" section is especially important for senior candidates because it shows how the person scaled beyond individual execution.

## Interview compression

Maintain multiple versions of major stories:

- 30-second proof point
- 90-second answer
- 3-minute deep dive

The facts should remain consistent across versions.

## Development feedback

Performance reviews often contain constructive criticism.

Use it to prepare for:

- weakness questions
- leadership-growth questions
- examples of learning
- possible reference-check themes

Do not automatically put development feedback into resumes or proactive positioning.

Look for repeated themes across years before treating feedback as a durable pattern.

## Confidentiality

Translate confidential internal details into externally understandable outcomes.

Prefer:

> Built a governed workflow that reduced manual cycle time by roughly 80%.

over proprietary implementation detail that is unnecessary to prove the achievement.
