# Compensation Strategy

## Objective

Compensation strategy should determine whether a move is economically worthwhile and prevent late-stage surprises.

## Separate the components

Always distinguish:

- base salary
- target bonus
- historical/expected bonus reliability
- commissions, if relevant
- annual cash
- equity grant
- equity vesting schedule
- sign-on bonus
- retention awards
- benefits
- severance
- other material compensation

## Dependable annual cash

A useful planning concept is dependable annual cash.

A default calculation is:

**Base salary + realistically dependable annual cash incentive**

Do not automatically count:

- one-time sign-on bonuses
- unusually high historical bonus payouts
- speculative private-company equity
- discretionary awards
- unvested equity that depends on remaining employed

## Establish guardrails

Where data supports it, define:

- current base
- current target bonus
- current dependable annual cash
- ideal base
- base floor
- ideal annual cash
- annual cash floor
- ideal total compensation
- total compensation floor
- desired dollar increase
- desired percentage increase

## Recruiter conversations

The objective is to establish alignment without unnecessarily anchoring to the candidate's current pay.

A useful pattern:

> I'm primarily evaluating the role based on scope and level. For the right move, I'm targeting compensation consistent with the market for this level and responsibility. Can you share the budgeted range and mix for the role?

When a target range is strategically useful, provide one grounded in current market evidence and the candidate's move criteria.

## Offer analysis

For offers, model at least:

### Year 1
Base + target bonus + sign-on + expected vesting

### Steady state
Base + target bonus + normal annual equity vesting

### Downside case
Base + conservative bonus + conservative equity realization

### Upside case
Base + target/upside bonus + reasonable equity appreciation assumptions

Label assumptions clearly.

## Negotiation priorities

Prioritize the components that matter most to the user's objective.

Common levers:

- base
- level/title
- target bonus
- sign-on
- equity
- first-year guaranteed bonus
- remote classification
- travel expectations
- start date
- severance
- review/promotion timing

Do not negotiate every possible term simply because it exists.
